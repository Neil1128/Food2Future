Waste Segregation


Waste segregation is the process of separating different types of waste materials based on their composition and characteristics. It involves categorizing waste into different streams, such as organic waste, recyclable materials, hazardous waste, and non-recyclable waste. The goal of waste segregation is to facilitate proper disposal, recycling, and treatment of waste, minimizing environmental impact and promoting sustainability.

By segregating waste at the source, it becomes easier to manage and process different types of waste effectively. Organic waste can be composted to create nutrient-rich soil, while recyclable materials can be sent for recycling to conserve resources. Hazardous waste requires special handling to prevent harm to human health and the environment. Non-recyclable waste can be disposed of in an appropriate manner, such as through incineration or landfill.

Waste segregation not only helps in reducing the amount of waste that ends up in landfills but also promotes resource conservation and environmental protection. It encourages responsible waste management practices and raises awareness about the importance of sustainable living.

Reusing sorted materials saves natural resources and uses less energy than making brand-new items
